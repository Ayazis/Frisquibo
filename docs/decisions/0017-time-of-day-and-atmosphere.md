# 0015 — Time of day and atmosphere: a physical sky, our own clouds, light from the same model

Date: 2026-11-18
Status: Accepted. Checked headless (import, `atmosphere_test`, `atmosphere_smoke`); the look still
needs a desktop run and tuning on a GPU (see "To check on a desktop").

## Context

The demo was lit by a `ProceduralSkyMaterial` gradient, one sun at two fixed angles (the T key), thin
distance fog and ACES. The sky had no clouds, the sun's colour was hand-picked, and the sky's ambient
light was strong for the sun (flat, low-contrast shadows). Lighting is the biggest lever on how real a
scene looks (ARCHITECTURE.md section 7), and FEATURE_MAP Track 1 asks for a time-of-day and weather
director that drives sun, sky, fog and exposure together from one data asset per weather type.

## Decisions

1. **`world/time_of_day/` is the feature.** `TimeOfDay` (node) is a solar clock with a day of the
   year, a latitude and a north heading; `SunPath` (pure) turns those into sun, moon and star
   directions. `Atmosphere` (node) builds and drives the sky, the environment, the sun and moon lights
   and lightning. Weather is data: `SkyWeather` resources in `world/time_of_day/weather/` (clear, fair,
   mist, overcast, storm), blended over a few seconds when it changes.
2. **One physical model of the air, on the CPU and in the sky shader.** Rayleigh and Mie scattering
   and ozone absorption over a round earth (Bruneton's coefficients), the Chapman function for the
   light's path (Schüler), and an even share for multiple scattering. `AtmosphereModel` (GDScript) and
   `sky_atmosphere.gdshaderinc` are the same maths with the same constants, and `atmosphere_test.gd`
   fails if the constants drift apart. The sun light's colour and strength are the transmittance of
   that air; the earth's shadow gives real twilight (clouds and the upper sky stay lit after sunset).
   The sky's brightness relative to the sun follows from the physics, so shadows are deep and blue.
3. **Our own volumetric clouds rather than an addon for now.** A cumulus layer ray-marched in the sky
   shader over 3D noise (`SkyNoise`: Perlin shapes, cellular erosion, generated by the engine), lit by
   a short march towards the sun with a multiple-scattering approximation (Wrenninge) and a two-lobe
   phase (silver linings), sunk into the haze with distance; plus a cirrus sheet, stars, and the moon
   with its phase. Sky3D and SunshineClouds2 (FEATURE_MAP candidates) stay candidates: adopting one
   needs a `prototypes/` comparison on a GPU first, and ours shares the CPU model so lights, clouds and
   sky agree. The clouds and air are drawn in Godot's half-resolution sky pass; the sharp sun and moon
   discs and stars at full resolution, behind the clouds' transmittance. The radiance map (ambient
   light and reflections) gets clouds with fewer samples and no discs (the lights stand in for those),
   updated incrementally because the clouds drift.
4. **Exposure from the light, not from the screen.** `AtmosphereLighting` estimates the radiance of a
   mid-grey scene (level and upright faces) and exposes for it, adapting only 70% of the way and at
   most 7 times the noon exposure, as the eye does: golden hour is darker than noon, night is night.
   Deterministic, so visual checks repeat. (Godot's auto-exposure measures the frame; it may still be
   worth trying once a physical camera is set up, Track 1.)
5. **Filmic look:** AgX tone mapping (sunsets stay orange), additive glow above white only, SSAO and
   SSIL tuned for outdoor scale, volumetric fog for light shafts near the camera, distance fog with
   the sky's own colour (aerial perspective), soft sun shadows of the sun's real size (PCSS) over four
   cascades, grading per weather, and debanding on (project setting) so the smooth sky and fog
   gradients don't band.
6. **Quality tiers:** `data/quality/atmosphere_{low,medium,high,ultra}.tres` hold every scalable
   number (sky steps, cloud steps, radiance map size, volumetric fog grid and reach, shadow distance,
   SSAO/SSIL, soft shadows). Desktop defaults to High; the web (Compatibility) uses Low, has no
   half-resolution sky pass (the shader draws everything in the full pass there), no volumetric fog
   and no screen-space effects.
7. **Only the brighter of sun and moon casts shadows**: two shadowed directional lights would split
   the shadow map.
8. **No SDFGI yet.** It needs the moving water meshes kept out of the GI voxels and a look on a GPU;
   SSAO/SSIL and the sky's ambient carry the indirect light until then.

## In the demo

The sky turns so the sun sets over the sea in front of the Beach view. T steps through dawn, morning,
noon, afternoon, golden hour, sunset, dusk and night; Y runs a time lapse (a day in about 96 s); V
steps through the weathers; each sea state brings its weather (calm: clear, storm: storm clouds and
lightning). The visual-check state takes `time`, `hour` and `weather` (and still `golden_hour`).

## To check on a desktop

- Overall level: `EXPOSURE_KEY` (atmosphere_lighting.gd), the cloud brightness
  (`CLOUD_SCATTER_GAIN`, sky_clouds.gdshaderinc) and the night (`MOON_IRRADIANCE`, `MAX_GAIN`).
- Cost at 1440p: the half-resolution cloud march runs over the whole screen, again in the pond and sea
  mirrors, and in the radiance map; heaviest under full overcast. The steps are in the quality tiers.
- The planar mirrors copy the environment, SSAO, SSIL and volumetric fog included: profile whether the
  mirrors need them.
- The far sea drawn by the sky below the horizon should meet the end of the ocean mesh without a seam.

## Consequences

- Scenes light themselves by adding a `TimeOfDay` and an `Atmosphere`; the prototypes keep their own
  simple lights.
- Shot lists made before this change will all differ: make new baselines.
